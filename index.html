A student guide to digital media literacy

Think Before You Share

School-related rumors can spread quickly online. Before treating a post as fact, check its source, date, and context—and consider the people who could be harmed by sharing it.

Use the sharing checklist → See the response plan

Remember: A screenshot, repost, or high number of shares is not proof. Verify claims before sharing. If a post suggests an immediate threat, contact trusted school staff and local emergency authorities without waiting for online confirmation.

Why verification matters

Unverified claims about school safety or student discipline can cause fear, harm reputations, and disrupt a community. Posts may lose important context when copied, cropped, or forwarded. Media and information literacy guidance encourages readers to evaluate sources and context before sharing.[1]

Check the claim, not just the post

Separate what a photo, screenshot, or video actually shows from what its caption says. Look for the original publication, confirm when and where it appeared, and compare it with independent reliable sources. Lateral reading—checking what other sources say about a publisher—can help assess unfamiliar websites and accounts.[2]

Why rumors can feel convincing

Urgent wording, emotional images, and repeated sharing can make a claim seem credible before anyone has checked it. Popularity measures attention, not accuracy. FactCheck.org offers more guidance on spotting questionable online claims.[3]

If you shared something inaccurate

Correct or remove the post and direct readers to reliable, updated information. Avoid repeating the harmful claim unnecessarily, and do not name, identify, or blame students.

A responsible response plan

Use these steps when you come across a concerning school-related post:

  1. Pause. Do not repost, speculate, or identify a student while a claim is unverified.
  2. Find the source. Look for the original post and note who published it, when, and what evidence it provides.
  3. Corroborate. Check official school or education-authority channels and reputable independent news coverage.
  4. Protect privacy. Do not spread students' names, photos, screenshots, or private details.
  5. Act on urgent concerns. Tell a trusted adult or school staff member and contact local emergency authorities about a possible immediate threat. Do not wait for online confirmation.

Warning signs to investigate

These clues do not prove a post is false. They are reasons to pause and seek reliable confirmation before sharing.

📸

Undated screenshots

A screenshot has no date, source link, or context.

Look for the original announcement and confirm when it was published.

📢

Anonymous urgent warnings

A forwarded message describes danger but gives no accountable source.

Do not forward it. Report immediate safety concerns to trusted adults and authorities.

🛡️

Unsupported accusations

A post blames a student without verified evidence.

Do not speculate about identity. Respect privacy and leave investigations to responsible authorities.

Before you share

Ask yourself these questions. If you cannot verify the key details, do not share the claim as fact.

  • Who is the original source? Is it identifiable and accountable?
  • When and where did this happen? Could an old post or image be presented as new?
  • What evidence supports the claim? Look beyond a dramatic caption or anonymous message.
  • Can an independent source confirm it? Reposts of the same claim are not independent confirmation.
  • Could sharing cause harm? Protect students' privacy and avoid amplifying unsupported accusations.

When in doubt: Keep the post private and ask a trusted adult or school staff member for guidance. Report urgent safety concerns directly.

Where to check current information

  • School announcements: Use the school's official website, verified social accounts, or established messaging channels.
  • Education updates: Check DepEd's official website or the relevant local education office.
  • Immediate safety concerns: Contact school staff and local emergency authorities directly.

Important: This guide cannot assess a real-time threat. Follow official instructions and local emergency guidance.
